How to measure

  1. Import the radiograph and calibrate using a known length (or enter mm/pixel).
  2. Mark, in order: mesial CEJ, distal CEJ, Point A (lowest point of the chamber roof), Point B (highest point of the chamber floor), and the radiographic apex of the longest root.
  3. On touch screens, press and hold: a magnifier appears above your finger. Slide to the exact spot and release. Drag any point later to refine it.
  4. Keyboard: hold Space and drag to move the image (hand tool). Click a point to select it, then use the arrow keys to nudge it (Shift = ×10). Delete removes the selected point.
  5. The final type appears in a pop-up on the image. Drag its title bar to move it, ✕ to close it, or 🔔 Result to bring it back.
  6. Multiple teeth: enter the Patient ID, tap a premolar or molar on the Palmer tooth chart (numbered from the midline: 4–5 premolars, 6–8 molars) and mark its 5 landmarks. Tap another tooth and repeat. Each tooth keeps its own points on the same image, and the chart shows each result at a glance. Save one tooth or all measured teeth at once; saving again updates the record instead of duplicating it.
  7. Check the classification, then save it to the dataset.

Definitions

V1: Point A to Point B (pulp chamber height).
V2: Point A to the apex of the longest root.
V3: perpendicular distance from Point B to the line joining the mesial and distal CEJ.
TI = (V1 ÷ V2) × 100.

A tooth is taurodontic only if TI ≥ 20 % and V3 ≥ 2.5 mm. Hypotaurodontism: 20–29.9 %. Mesotaurodontism: 30–39.9 %. Hypertaurodontism: ≥ 40 %.

Reference: Shifman A, Chanannel I. Community Dent Oral Epidemiol. 1978;6(4):200–203.
